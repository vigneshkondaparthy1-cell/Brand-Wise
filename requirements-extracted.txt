At a glance
Question Proposal
Who uses it? Creators (employees, agencies, freelancers), reviewers, brand managers and
admins.
What is reviewed? Text, images and documents; tone, terminology, messaging, claims, visuals and
accessibility.
What happens to
findings?
Each shows location, explanation, severity, approved source, confidence and
suggested fix; a person accepts, edits or dismisses it.
How is work
governed?
Role-based comments, approval states, version comparison, audit history and
insights on repeat issues.
What makes it
trustworthy?
Approved-source grounding, explicit uncertainty, private workspaces, human
sign-off and disclosed AI services.
Problem in brief
Growing organisations publish through multiple teams, agencies and AI tools. Unsupported
claims, old facts, inconsistent language and off-brand visuals can reach the public before
review. Brand checks often depend on remembering long PDFs and fragmented email or chat
approvals.
Scope and principle
First version: pre-publication content checks with source citations; suggested edits; roles,
comments, approvals, history and repeat-issue insights. Not initially: auto-posting, video
review, custom AI training, automated final approval, enterprise SSO or billing.

01 UNDERSTANDING THE PROBLEM
The challenge is to detect mistakes before publication without turning AI into a decision-maker.
Reviewers need reliable evidence, creators need actionable feedback, and managers need to
learn which guidelines cause repeated confusion.
Failure Example and consequence
Unsupported claims “Fastest in the market” without approved proof: legal and credibility risk.
Outdated facts Old pricing, discontinued features or former product names mislead
customers.
Inconsistent language Different agencies use varying product terms or tone, fragmenting brand
identity.
Off-brand visuals Wrong logo, colours or low-contrast image text hurt consistency and
accessibility.
Unchecked AI content Invented facts in AI-written drafts can multiply errors quickly.
Users and setting
Assumed initial environment: a growing organisation of about 50–500 employees with
marketing, sales and support teams plus at least two external agencies. Brand guidance is held
in PDFs/slides; drafts originate in docs, design tools and AI assistants; approvals often happen
through email or chat. These assumptions require validation.

02 PROPOSED SOLUTION & WORKFLOW
Value proposition: catch brand errors early, explain them against approved evidence, preserve
the author’s intent and give final authority to designated people.
Core design principles
• Grounded: cite approved facts, guidelines or assets; if evidence is absent or expired, say
“cannot verify - needs human check”, not an invented answer.
• Explainable: show location, category, severity, why flagged, evidence, confidence and a
suggested rewrite.
• Intent-preserving: corrections appear as editable suggestions; no silent changes.
• Human-led and private: role-based approvals, recorded decisions, organisation-level isolation
and AI disclosures.
• Hybrid: deterministic checks for terms, contrast and measurable specifications; AI for tone,
messaging and nuanced claims.
Nine-stage review flow
Stage Action and responsible party
1. Brand setup Brand manager imports rules, approved facts/claims, terms, logos and assets;
assigns owners and review/expiry dates.
2. Submission Creator uploads text, image, PDF or slide content; selects channel and type (draft
v1).
3. Automated
check
System parses input, retrieves applicable approved sources and runs tone, claim,
terminology, visual and accessibility checks.
4. Findings System produces cited flags with severity, confidence and suggested fixes.
5. Revision Creator accepts, edits or dismisses suggestions and records reasons (draft v2).
6. Human review Reviewer or manager comments, compares versions and resolves flags.
7. Decision Authorized reviewer/manager approves or requests changes, sending it back to
revision.
8. Publication Creator/manager publishes the approved version; its record and audit trail remain.
9. Insights Manager studies repeated issues, dismissals and ambiguous guidelines, then
updates sources.
States: Draft → Auto-checked → In review → Changes requested (if needed) → Approved → Published.
Rejection loops back to revision; publication itself is not automated in Phase 1.

Distinctive capabilities
A claim ledger tracks approved wording, evidence, owners and expiry; a cannot-verify state
communicates uncertainty; and unclear-guideline detection highlights rules frequently disputed
or dismissed so managers can improve the source guidance, not only the draft.
03 FEATURES & CHECKS
Prioritised delivery
Priority Capabilities
Phase 1 / Core Import guidelines (text, PDF, DOCX), facts, terms and banned phrases;
approved-claim ledger with evidence/owner/expiry; text analysis of tone,
terminology and claims with citations; highlighted, explained fixes;
Creator/Reviewer/Brand manager/Admin roles; six-state approval process;
comments, comparison and version history.
Phase 2 Image and accessibility review (logo, palette, contrast, alt text, readability and
heading structure); dashboard by team/agency with trends and ambiguous rules;
choose one Google Docs add-on or Slack notification integration.
Optional stretch Video transcript review; Canva or Figma integration.
Checks and techniques
Dimension Method
Tone and voice AI model guided by organisation-specific tone and style rules.
Terminology Approved names, prohibited/discouraged words: deterministic rules plus AI.
Messaging and claims Retrieve relevant approved facts/claim ledger entries, then AI checks
consistency and cites evidence.
Visual consistency Vision model plus deterministic checks for logos, fonts, palettes and visual
style.
Accessibility Rule-based contrast, alt-text, reading-level and heading-structure checks.
Illustrative finding
Text: “Cuts onboarding time by 30%” (paragraph 2). Type: unsupported claim; high severity.
Reason: approved claim is 20% and no approved source supports 30%. Evidence: ledger entry
including owner and expiry. Suggestion: “Cuts onboarding time by up to 20%”. State:
source-verified, or “cannot verify - needs human check”. Action: accept, edit or dismiss with
optional explanation.

Use the organisation's evidence

Separate checks from judgement

Reliability of AI findings
Require an approved source ID for source-backed findings, otherwise drop the flag or label it
unverified. Run measurable checks as rules; display confidence and require human judgement.
Evaluate on a labelled test set, count wrong flags and provide a “report incorrect flag” control
for improvement.
04 TECHNOLOGY PLAN
Architecture: React web interface → FastAPI authentication/API and review orchestration →
ingestion/parsing, approval workflow, retrieval and hybrid check engines →
PostgreSQL/pgvector for brand data, findings, comments, history and embeddings; optional
disclosed external LLM/multimodal APIs.
Layer Proposed implementation
Frontend React, TypeScript and Tailwind CSS for role-specific dashboards, uploads,
side-by-side edits, comments and version comparison.
Backend Python FastAPI with JWT-based authentication, role enforcement and
restricted agency access.
Database / retrieval PostgreSQL plus pgvector for tenant-scoped records, approved sources, claim
ledger, workflow history and semantic retrieval.
AI Structured-output LLM API (e.g., Claude) for tone/claim reasoning; multimodal
model for images. Exact services to be selected and disclosed.
Rules / parsing WCAG contrast, terminology lists; pypdf, python-docx and Tesseract OCR for
document ingestion.
Deployment Vercel frontend; Render or Railway backend; public GitHub repository and
shareable demo.
Inputs, integrations and test data
• Demo: fictional brand guidelines and fact sheet, avoiding confidential material.
• Evaluation: labelled examples of both compliant and noncompliant posts/images.
• Standards/integrations: WCAG for accessibility; one Google Docs or Slack integration in Phase
2.
• Customer data: organisation-isolated uploads that are not used to train models.
System guardrails
Check source permissions during retrieval, validate file uploads, store review and approval
decisions, and label the AI model/service responsible for recommendations. Separate
deterministic results from subjective AI opinions; avoid implying unsupported claims are
proven.

05 RESPONSIBLE DESIGN
Concern Required safeguard
Privacy Tenant isolation; encryption in transit and at rest; retention settings and
deletion on request; no model training on customer content.
Security Role-based access and restricted agency views; audit trail, upload validation
and secrets excluded from Git.
Human oversight Suggestions never apply automatically; approvals by authorised humans;
acceptance, edits and dismissal logged.
Transparency Label AI output; list models and external services in interface and README;
cite sources for findings.
Reliability Source IDs, explicit uncertainty, confidence display, labelled evaluations and
incorrect-flag reporting.
Fairness / language Judge tone against organisation-authored policy, not model preference; test
varied styles including Indian English.
Accessibility Check published content and make the product keyboard-accessible with
labels and sufficient contrast.
Licensing / IP Disclose open-source libraries/templates; use original or openly licensed
demo assets.
AI disclosure for this proposal
Document and interface should identify AI models/services used by the product. Complete the
original proposal placeholder with the actual AI tools used to prepare the proposal: [list AI tools

Practical trust test
The product must not confidently invent an approved claim. For each flag, a reviewer should
see the exact source and its status, assess confidence, change or reject the suggestion and
reconstruct what happened from the audit log.

Expected impact and evidence
• Fewer publication errors: share of seeded mistakes detected before approval.
• Faster reviews: compare time per draft with and without  in a small study; record
participant count.
• Trust: percentage of flags backed by a valid approved source and reviewer-reported false
flags.
• Better rules and onboarding: number of ambiguous guidelines clarified; time until a new
agency creator submits a compliant draft.
Expected benefits are reduced rework for creators, more time for reviewer judgement, better manager
visibility and lower credibility/compliance risk. Report measured results rather than inventing
improvement percentages.
